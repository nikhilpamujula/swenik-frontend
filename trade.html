Blind. Normally leg 2 is sized to leg 1's real fill, which costs a status query — and WazirX allows 1.6 of those per second with a burst of ONE, so a 3-leg loop queues behind itself for about a second. Blind mode places leg 1 and goes straight to leg 2, sized to plan. If leg 1 filled, leg 2 works. If it did not, leg 2 is rejected for insufficient funds and nothing is lost. A PARTIAL fill is the real risk: leg 2 is rejected, then re-placed for whatever the exchange says you actually hold. Resting orders are cancelled after the window below. Only one intra loop rule runs at a time — enabling this disables the normal one, and vice versa.
Measured cost of asking: 73ms for the INR read, then ~1,250ms more for the USDT read waiting on a token the INR read had just taken — on a trade that otherwise takes 328ms. What you give up: "% of balance" stops meaning anything (size is max ₹/trade, full stop), and on path B the engine can no longer tell whether USDT is held, so it ALWAYS skips leg 1 and starts at coin/USDT. If the USDT is not there, leg 2 is rejected at entry — nothing acquired, recorded as a miss. What stays: the daily ₹ cap, trades/day, and the shrink-to-balance retry after an insufficient-funds rejection. 4 Sep: this now applies to EVERY strategy, not just intra loop. The account endpoint is 1 call/second, and a 429 there makes the client pause every class — HBAR order 2818 waited 1,635ms to place while the order bucket had 7 free tokens. A balance read rate-limited a trade, so the read is gone. The risk you take in exchange: max ₹/trade becomes the ONLY thing between the plan and a WazirX rejection. On 3 Sep that produced seven dead DGB orders in 128 seconds because max ₹/trade was ₹40,000 against a ₹37,668 balance. Keep max ₹/trade below your working balance. On Direct + hedge and Loop the stakes are higher than on intra loop: those end holding a position, so a mis-sized trade leaves something behind rather than nothing.

Observed fills land in 141-151ms, so 800 is already generous.

Every plan reads two books (coin/INR + coin/USDT for Intra loop; WazirX + Binance for Direct). This picks which one the age limit above is measured against.
min and off are close to the same thing. The engine re-evaluates because one of the two just ticked, so the freshest is almost always ~0ms — and on Direct, Binance streams continuously. Choose min when you accept that a quiet book is still a valid book and you only want the limit to catch a total feed outage.
max is the original behaviour: one quiet book holds back the whole triangle. That is what made ZEC 2795 read 1801ms when the book that had just ticked was 0ms old.
Whichever you pick, both ages are always logged in the validated event (signalAges) with stalest, maxMs and minMs — so switching to min hides nothing, it only stops the older book from vetoing the trade.
how old the order books may be at the moment the engine decides — measured when the plan is built, checked before the order row is created, and never re-checked after that. Your orders land ~90–110ms later (ZEC 91ms, BRISE 111ms), so at 8000+ that gap is noise; at 2000 it was 5% of the budget. Blank = 2000ms.
An unchanged book is not a stale book. WazirX sends depth only when the book changes, so a quiet pair simply stops ticking — the resting orders are still there. A 20-minute-old zecusdt against a fresh zecinr is a real triangle, and a day-old WazirX ask that Binance just repriced 10% away is the best Direct setup there is, not the worst. Age is not error, and this number cannot tell "nobody moved it" apart from "the update never arrived".

The honest caveat: a phantom arb still exists. A paper HNT triangle once planned +₹25,366 on a 6041ms book while that bid collapsed 0.281 → 0.196. But the defence is confirming the level (or refusing thin ones), not refusing old ones.
OFF by default — this opens a REAL futures position on its own initiative. When a leg fails and the trade is left holding the coin, the engine shorts that quantity on the coin's USDT perp. Holding USDT or INR is never hedged (no meaningful price risk).
A hedge freezes the loss, it does not recover it. The position becomes delta-neutral at whatever it was already worth; funding accrues, and both sides still need unwinding by hand.
Refused — and the breaker still trips at once — when the coin has no Binance perp (HNT is one), when that perp already has an open position (one-way mode: a sell would reduce it, not hedge), under 5× leverage, or with futures unfunded.
The breaker only relaxes if the hedge actually landed. An unhedged strand still disarms the rule on the first failure. Try it on a Paper rule first — paper simulates the hedge and sends no futures order.
